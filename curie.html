Marie Curie

  • The pioneer of radioactivity and the first woman to win a Nobel Prize.

    • Biography

  • Marie Curie (1867–1934) was a Polish-born physicist and chemist who conducted pioneering research on radioactivity. She was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize and the only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different scientific fields (Physics and Chemistry).

  • She conducted most of her research in France, where she worked with her husband Pierre Curie. Together, they discovered new radioactive elements and changed the understanding of atomic science forever.

    • Major Discoveries & Contributions

    1. Discovery of Polonium
    2. Discovery of Radium
    3. Research on Radioactivity
    4. Development of X-ray units during WWI
    5. Foundation of modern nuclear physics

    Timeline of Marie Curie’s Life

    Year Event
    1867 Born in Warsaw, Poland
    1891 Moved to Paris to study at Sorbonne
    1898 Discovered Polonium and Radium
    1903 Won Nobel Prize in Physics
    1911 Won Nobel Prize in Chemistry
    1934 Died due to radiation exposure

    Fun Facts

    1. She coined the term “radioactivity”.
    2. She carried radioactive materials in her pockets.
    3. Her notebooks are still radioactive today.
    4. She founded the Curie Institutes in Paris and Warsaw.
    5. She refused to patent her discoveries for profit.
